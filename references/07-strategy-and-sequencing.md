# 07 · Strategy: sequence the plan, then focus

## The Secret Master Plan (2006)

> "The strategy of Tesla is to enter at the high end of the market, where customers are prepared to pay a
> premium, and then drive down market as fast as possible to higher unit volume and lower prices with each
> successive model." [MP1]

> "Almost any new technology initially has high unit cost before it can be optimized and this is no less true
> for electric cars." [MP1]

His four-line summary, as written in the post [MP1]:

> Build sports car
> Use that money to build an affordable car
> Use that money to build an even more affordable car
> While doing above, also provide zero emission electric power generation options
> Don't tell anyone.

> "In keeping with a fast growing technology company, all free cash flow is plowed back into R&D to drive down
> the costs and bring the follow on products to market as fast as possible." [MP1]

## Part Deux (2016): explain the bigger picture

> "However, the main reason was to explain how our actions fit into a larger picture, so that they would seem
> less random." [MP2]

> "Without economies of scale, anything we built would be expensive, whether it was an economy sedan or a sports
> car." [MP2]

His honest odds at the start: "I thought our chances of success were so low that I didn't want to risk anyone's
funds in the beginning but my own." [MP2] And: "Starting a car company is idiotic and an electric car company is
idiocy squared." [MP2]

(Tesla's Master Plan Part 3 (2023) and Part IV (2025) are signed by "The Tesla Team", not by Musk, so they are
not quoted as his words here.)

## The tech tree

> "So, there's kind of a tech tree, if you will. You've got the basics. You need literacy before you can have
> Lord of the Rings." [LEX438 00:16:18]

Start with the problem you can solve now: "The logical thing to do is… Sensible thing to do is to start off
solving basic neuron damage issues." [LEX438 00:17:58]

## Focus ruthlessly on the next milestone

> "We're gonna focus on getting to orbit. We don't need a door." [SB2 28:01 to 28:03]

> "We need to be super focused on getting to orbit then super focused on getting the ship back, then we can
> worry about doors." [SB2 28:06 to 28:12]

> "It's just an unnecessary complexity. Is the door necessary to solve the problem? No." [SB2 28:15 to 28:19]

## Nest the goals, measure them in time

> "The overarching optimization is... fastest time to a city on Mars and then subset... fastest time to a fully
> usable [sic: reusable] rocket, subset fastest time to orbit." [SB3 16:42]

**The move:** one top goal, with each sub-goal written as "fastest time to X".

## Only think about what you have to think about now

> "For now we're just trying to think about the things that we have to think about." [SB3 04:14]

## Pick problems that matter

> "I thought about, what are the problems that are most likely to affect the future of the world or the future
> of humanity?" [TED13]

> "A measure of success, I'd say, how many useful things can I get done?" [LEX438 01:17:58]
