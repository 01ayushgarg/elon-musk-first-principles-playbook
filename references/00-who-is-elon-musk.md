# 00 · Who Elon Musk is, for this playbook

This playbook is about one thing: how Elon Musk says he thinks about engineering, manufacturing, cost, speed and
building companies. It doesn't cover his politics, his personal life or his social media disputes. Where an
interview drifts into those, we skip that part entirely.

## His mission statements, briefly

In 2006 he wrote that the purpose of Tesla, and his reason for funding it, was to help move the economy from
burning hydrocarbons to solar electricity [MP1]. In 2013 he restated the goal as "to accelerate the advent of
sustainable transport by bringing compelling mass market electric cars to market as soon as possible" [MIS13].
For SpaceX: "So for SpaceX, the goal is to establish a self-sustaining city on Mars." [LEX438 01:22:19]

Every method in this playbook serves a goal like these. **Our reading:** if you can't write your own goal in one
line, start with chapter 07 before anything else.

## Read him with his own caveats

> "I would say that I'm pathologically optimistic on schedule. This is true." [LEX400 01:54:38]

He also says he has "personally made the mistake of going backwards on all five steps multiple times" of his own
algorithm [SB1 22:02], and that the 2018 Model 3 line was "messed up" almost everywhere [TED22 33:46]. The
playbook keeps those admissions in, because the failures are where the methods came from.

## How to read the chapters

- **His words** are in quotation marks with a source ID. Nothing else is.
- **Our reading** marks our interpretation or a translation from rockets and cars to software, services or a
  small team.
- **Our suggestion** marks steps, thresholds, templates and numbers that are ours, not his.
- **Fictional** marks invented scenarios. Every worked number in the chapters is invented unless it is quoted.

Take the methods. Treat his timelines as aspirations.
